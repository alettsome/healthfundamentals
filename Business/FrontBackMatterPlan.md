# Front And Back Matter Plan

## Purpose

This document defines the supporting material that should sit around the main chapters of *Health Fundamentals*.

The goal is not to pad the book.
The goal is to make the book feel more complete, more credible, and more actionable.

## Recommended Front Matter

### 1. Half Title

Optional.
Useful if you want a more traditional print presentation.

### 2. Title Page

Include:

- title
- subtitle
- author name

### 3. Copyright Page

Include standard publishing information.

### 4. Preface Or Opening Note

This is strongly recommended.

It should do four jobs:

- explain why this book was written
- acknowledge the confusion people feel around fragmented wellness advice
- state the book's natural, affordable, whole-food conviction clearly
- invite readers to test, observe, and think rather than blindly accept

This section is also the cleanest place to establish your tone of honesty:

- this is not about perfection
- this is not about expensive protocols
- this is not about forcing debate
- this is about helping readers see a practical framework more clearly

### 5. How To Use This Book

Optional, but helpful.

Keep it short.
One or two pages is enough.

It can explain:

- the building-block order
- why the chapters move from foundation to integration to sustainability
- how readers can move through the book without feeling overwhelmed

## Recommended Back Matter

### 1. Closing Reflection

This is already consistent with the direction of the manuscript.

It should leave the reader with:

- clarity
- dignity
- agency
- a sense that small, steady action still matters

### 2. References And Notes

This is strongly recommended.

Even if the book is not written in an academic tone, references and notes make the work feel more grounded and more responsible.

This section can include:

- supporting books
- articles
- organizations
- public health guidance
- selected research references where appropriate

Best approach:

- keep the main chapters readable
- use light in-text mention where natural
- place fuller references in the back

That supports credibility without making the prose feel heavy.

### 3. Appendix A: Food Foundation Builder

This appendix can translate the whole book into a simple practical framework.

Possible contents:

- hydration base ideas
- mineral-rich food examples
- vitamin-rich food examples
- protein anchors
- essential fat supports
- simple combinations that bring the pieces together

### 4. Appendix B: Blender And Preparation Guide

This is a strong fit for the book's real-life promise.

Possible contents:

- how to think about blender choices by budget and lifestyle
- basic smoothie-building logic
- texture and taste balancing ideas
- fast prep and cleanup habits
- what matters most in a blender and what does not

### 5. Appendix C: Buying And Cleaning Basics

This can cover practical concerns readers often have but do not want expanded into full chapters.

Possible contents:

- buying on a budget
- when organic matters more
- when flexibility is reasonable
- produce cleaning basics
- simple low-cost kitchen habits

### 6. Companion Access Note

This should appear in the back matter even if it is also mentioned earlier.

Purpose:

- give readers a clear path to the buyer companion
- create the bridge from Amazon or retailer purchase to your website
- reinforce that your website is the book's living home base

## Naming Guidance

Avoid the word `worksheets` unless the material becomes very structured and workbook-like.

Better naming directions:

- field guide
- action companion
- practical companion
- reader guide
- field pages
- checklists
- reflection pages

## Suggested Lean Structure

If you want the cleanest version, the best supporting package is:

1. Preface / Opening Note
2. How To Use This Book
3. Closing Reflection
4. References / Notes
5. Appendix A: Food Foundation Builder
6. Appendix B: Blender And Preparation Guide
7. Companion Access Note

## Best Recommendation

For this book, the strongest supporting architecture is:

- a brief but conviction-rich opening note
- a short "how to use this book" page
- a closing reflection
- a real references / notes section
- two to three practical appendices
- a clear companion access page that points readers to your website

That gives the book more solidity without making it bloated.
