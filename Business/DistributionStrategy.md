# Distribution Strategy

## Purpose

This document weighs the practical trade-offs between:

- selling through Amazon
- selling directly through a simple website
- using a hybrid approach

It also covers:

- low-budget implementation options
- buyer-only bonus resource ideas
- ways to verify purchase without relying on a generic email-for-freebie model
- how Amazon readers can be guided to your website

## Core Question

Should the book be sold primarily through Amazon, directly through your own website, or through both?

## Option 1: Amazon-Only

### Pros

- built-in buyer trust
- familiar checkout flow
- large discovery engine
- easier fulfillment expectations for readers
- easier review collection
- easier print distribution if using Amazon's print systems

### Cons

- lower margins
- weaker ownership of the customer relationship
- less control over branding and post-purchase experience
- limited ability to bundle strong buyer-only companion resources
- dependence on Amazon's rules, algorithms, and platform priorities

## Option 2: Direct-Only Website

### Pros

- higher margins per sale
- full control of brand, copy, design, and buyer experience
- easier bundling of bonus resources
- direct control over pricing and positioning
- better long-term business ownership

### Cons

- no built-in discovery
- lower immediate trust than Amazon for cold traffic
- more operational responsibility
- more work around payments, delivery, support, and refunds
- physical-book fulfillment becomes more complicated on a tight budget

## Option 3: Hybrid Model

### What It Means

Use Amazon as a trust and discovery channel while also having your own site for:

- direct sales
- stronger margins
- brand ownership
- buyer-only bonus resources
- future offers and companion materials

### Pros

- best balance of discovery and ownership
- Amazon can catch readers who want familiar checkout
- direct site can serve loyal readers and higher-value buyers
- easier to test what converts without betting everything on one channel

### Cons

- slightly more complexity
- requires clearer messaging so readers understand where to buy and why

## Strategic Recommendation

If budget is tight and the goal is realistic momentum, the strongest approach is:

### Hybrid, But Simple

Use:

- Amazon as an optional purchase path
- your own one-page site as your real home base

This avoids making Amazon your identity while still letting it serve readers who trust it.

In plain language:

do not build your business on Amazon alone, but do not refuse Amazon if it helps discovery and trust.

## Important Reality About Amazon Traffic

Amazon does **not** naturally send meaningful buyer traffic to your website for you.

If a reader buys on Amazon, you should assume that you must build the bridge yourself inside the book and around the brand.

That means the website handoff must be intentional.

## How To Bridge Amazon Buyers To Your Website

The cleanest path is not trying to force traffic out of Amazon before purchase.
The cleanest path is giving buyers a clear reason to visit your site after purchase.

## Best Bridge Mechanisms

### 1. Companion Access Page Inside The Book

Put a short page in the front matter or back matter that says, in substance:

- your buyer companion is available at your site
- direct buyers get it automatically
- retailer buyers can unlock it through the site

This gives the reader a reason to leave the book and visit your brand home.

### 2. Short Memorable URL

Use a simple page such as:

- `yourdomain.com/start`
- `yourdomain.com/companion`
- `yourdomain.com/book`

Do not rely on a long messy link.

### 3. Printed Access Word Or Code

Put a simple word or code inside the book that readers enter on your site.

This is not perfect security, but it is simple and low-friction.

### 4. QR Code As A Secondary Path

Add a QR code for readers who prefer scanning.
Keep the short URL printed as well.

### 5. Repeat The Bridge In More Than One Place

Best locations:

- preface or opening note
- closing reflection
- companion access page in the appendix or back matter

The same bridge should appear at least twice.

## What The Website Page Should Do

The landing page for book buyers should:

- welcome readers from the book
- explain the companion clearly
- separate direct buyers from Amazon or retailer buyers
- give direct buyers immediate access
- give retailer buyers a simple unlock path

It should not feel like a generic mailing-list trap.

## Best Practical Verification Flow

### Direct Buyers

- purchase on your site
- hosted checkout completes
- buyer lands on thank-you page
- companion is delivered immediately

### Amazon And Other Retail Buyers

Best low-friction path:

- visit the printed companion page URL
- enter the access word or code from the book
- reach the companion access page

Optional stronger backup:

- if needed, also provide a manual verification form using order number and retailer name

## Best Low-Budget Direct Model

If you want a simple direct setup without getting buried in platform complexity, the safest low-cost approach is:

### Website Layer

- a simple one-page site
- clean design
- clear offer
- one main call to action

### Payment Layer

- Stripe Checkout or another reputable hosted checkout

Why:

- strong trust
- lower complexity than custom cart logic
- less security burden than building payment handling yourself

### Delivery Layer

For digital resources:

- secure download page after purchase
- unique post-purchase access page
- or lightweight digital delivery integration

For physical books:

- either self-fulfill in small volume
- or keep physical fulfillment through Amazon/print partner while using your site mainly for branding and buyer bonuses

## Security Reality

If budget is tight, the safest move is usually not "build everything yourself."
The safest move is:

- own the brand and website
- use trusted hosted payment infrastructure
- avoid storing payment details yourself

That reduces both cost and security risk.

## What I Would Not Recommend Right Now

- building a fully custom ecommerce system from scratch
- relying on one expensive all-in-one platform before demand is proven
- making physical direct fulfillment the only path on day one unless you already have a simple fulfillment process
- assuming the Amazon author page alone will solve the website bridge

## Practical Bonus Resource Strategy

You mentioned wanting a buyer-only bonus that feels valuable, practical, and not overdone.

That is a strong instinct.

## Best Bonus Resource Types For This Book

### 1. Health Fundamentals Companion Field Guide

A short companion that helps readers act on the book immediately.

Could include:

- what to do first
- what to swap first
- what to reduce first
- what to track first

### 2. Budget Whole-Food Buying Guide

A practical grocery and staples guide:

- best foods to buy on a tight week
- what matters more to buy cleaner
- what can be more flexible
- simple low-cost combinations

### 3. Blender And Food-Builder Sheet

A very practical one-page tool:

- hydration base
- protein anchor
- mineral-rich add-ins
- fat support
- flavor upgrades

### 4. The Derailer Recovery Reset

A short guide for when readers fall off:

- bad week reset
- emotional eating reset
- what to do first after chaos

## Strongest Bonus Recommendation

The strongest buyer-only companion is probably:

### Health Fundamentals Companion Field Guide

with a section inside it for:

- budget buying
- blender building
- blender buying guidance
- reset after derailment
- practical first actions

That keeps the bonus simple, useful, and aligned with the whole book.

## Buyer Verification Without Generic Email Capture

You said you do not want the usual "give me your email for a freebie" model.
That makes sense.

Better options:

### Option 1: Bonus Access Code Inside The Book

Include a code or private URL inside the book.

Why it works:

- buyer-only
- simple
- no fake lead magnet feeling

### Option 2: Purchase Verification Page

Ask buyers for:

- order number
- retailer name
- maybe purchase date

Then give them bonus access manually or semi-manually.

Why it works:

- protects value better
- does not require broad free access

### Option 3: Direct Buyer Thank-You Page

For direct website purchases, send buyers to a private thank-you page with the companion resource immediately.

Why it works:

- simple
- clean
- no extra friction

## Recommendation On Bonus Access

Best practical system:

- direct buyers get the bonus immediately on the thank-you page
- Amazon buyers get access through a code or purchase-verification method inside the book

That keeps the bonus tied to the purchase without leaning on an uninspired email gate.

## Strategic Summary

### Best Current Business Path

1. Keep your own one-page website as the brand home
2. Use trusted checkout infrastructure instead of custom payment code
3. Let Amazon remain an optional purchase path, not your core identity
4. Offer one strong buyer-only bonus resource
5. Use book-code or order verification instead of generic free-email gating
6. Build the Amazon-to-website bridge into the book itself

## Next Decision Points

The next choices you need to make are:

1. Hybrid or direct-first?
2. Digital-only direct, or digital plus physical?
3. What is the first buyer-only companion resource?
4. What purchase verification method feels right?

## Recommendation

My recommendation is:

- hybrid model
- one-page website as main brand home
- Amazon as optional trust/discovery channel
- buyer-only digital companion resource
- code or order verification instead of open free download
- a clear printed bridge from the book to your site using a short URL, QR code, and companion access note
