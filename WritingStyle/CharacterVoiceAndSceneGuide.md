# Character Voice And Scene Guide

This guide exists to make later chapter revisions more consistent with the strongest parts of Chapter 1.

## Core Relationship

- Judy is steady, practical, encouraging, and grounded.
- Alex is thoughtful, curious, serious about what matters, and capable of light humor.
- Their exchanges should feel respectful, clear, and believable.

## Preferred Scene Types

Use scenes that naturally allow food, observation, and conversation:

- office lunch breaks
- break rooms
- lunchrooms
- park benches or picnic tables
- buffet lines
- casual restaurant settings
- potlucks
- church meals
- community gatherings
- grocery stores or markets

These scenes help the reader see food choices and compare patterns in a way that feels socially appropriate and easy to imagine.

## Scenes To Limit

Avoid relying on:

- Alex's apartment
- private in-home visits that feel too intimate
- overly domestic private settings unless there is a clear reason and the tone stays respectful

The default dynamic should feel public, grounded, and appropriate.

## Alex's Voice

Alex should sound like Chapter 1.

He is:

- sincere
- observant
- inquisitive
- occasionally funny
- not sarcastic
- not cynical
- not mocking the whole topic

## What Alex's Humor Sounds Like

His humor works best when it comes from:

- practical realism
- mild exaggeration
- curious comparison
- self-aware honesty

Examples of the kind of tone to preserve:

- "So my skeleton owes something to spinach. That is a lot of pressure for one leafy green."
- "If it comes in a gummy, I admit I am listening."
- "So an orange is basically a mechanic now?"
- "This is not about becoming a gym advertisement. It is about not quietly falling apart."

These lines are funny without making Alex cynical.

## Historical Context

When chapters use historical context:

- frame it as what used to be more common versus what dominates now
- emphasize whole foods, simple preparation, soups, broths, ferments, and slower methods
- connect that contrast to the book's thesis about fragmentation, confusion, and rising sickness
- keep it as clear framing, not as a formal history lecture

## Food Framing

- Show real meals and visible choices whenever possible.
- Use lunch containers, buffet spreads, potluck tables, salads, soups, eggs, beans, fruit, fish, greens, oats, yogurt, and similar practical foods.
- Let blending and juicing function as bridges that help modern people reconnect with whole-food patterns.
