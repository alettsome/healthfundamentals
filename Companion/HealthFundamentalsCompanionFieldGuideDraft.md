# Health Fundamentals Companion Field Guide

## How To Use This Companion

This companion is not meant to overwhelm you.
It is meant to help you act on the book in a simple, affordable, repeatable way.

Use it like a field guide:

- return to it when you need clarity
- use one section at a time
- circle what fits your life
- ignore perfection
- build rhythm before complexity

The goal is not to do everything.
The goal is to make the fundamentals easier to live.

## Start Here

If everything feels messy, begin with this order:

1. Improve water.
2. Build one steadier meal or blend.
3. Bring in a few affordable whole-food staples.
4. Reduce one obvious burden.
5. Repeat what is realistic before adding more.

If you do not know where to begin, begin with what you can repeat on a hard day.

## Your First Food Swaps

These are not strict rules.
They are simple examples of moving from scattered eating toward stronger foundations.

### Swap Direction 1

Instead of:

- skipping breakfast
- coffee only
- pastry only

Move toward:

- water first
- oats, eggs, yogurt, or a steadier blend

### Swap Direction 2

Instead of:

- random snacking with no structure

Move toward:

- one protein anchor
- one mineral-rich or fiber-rich food
- one source of lasting energy

### Swap Direction 3

Instead of:

- buying many small wellness products

Move toward:

- a few whole foods you will actually use this week

## Tight-Week Buying Guide

When money is tight, focus on value, not novelty.

### High-Value Basics

- oats
- eggs
- beans or lentils
- yogurt
- cabbage
- carrots
- frozen berries
- bananas
- sweet potatoes
- greens
- sardines or other affordable fish when suitable
- seeds

### What To Prioritize First

- water support
- one steady breakfast or blend base
- one protein anchor
- one or two reliable produce choices
- one mineral-supporting addition

### What Can Wait

- trend foods
- highly branded powders
- expensive wellness extras you will not use consistently

## Bulk Buying Tips

Bulk buying helps only when it reduces stress and waste.

Buy in larger amounts when the food is:

- shelf-stable
- freezable
- something you already use consistently
- meaningfully cheaper in larger volume

Good bulk candidates:

- oats
- beans
- lentils
- rice if it fits your approach
- seeds
- frozen produce
- herbs or chopped produce for freezing

Be careful bulk buying:

- delicate greens
- soft fruit
- niche products you are only "hoping" to start using

## Buying Through The Marketing Noise

Many products are sold with emotional language, but the useful questions are simpler.

Before buying, ask:

- What does this actually help me do?
- Will I use it more than once?
- Is it replacing a basic need or distracting me from one?
- Is the whole-food version cheaper and more practical?
- Am I buying this from clarity or from health anxiety?

## Water And Filters

You do not need the most expensive system to take water seriously.

### What To Look For In A Filter

- clear information on what it removes
- replacement filters that are easy to get
- maintenance that you will actually keep up with
- a long-term cost that still fits your budget
- a setup that matches your living situation

### Practical Reminder

The best filter is the one you will actually use and maintain.

If you ignore it, never change it, or resent the upkeep, it is not the right fit.

## Produce Buying Basics

You do not have to buy everything organic.
You do want to buy intentionally.

### A Practical Order

- buy the best you can afford consistently
- be more selective where produce tends to carry heavier residue concerns
- allow flexibility where budget is tight
- use washing and preparation to reduce unnecessary burden

The goal is not food fear.
The goal is wiser buying.

## Washing And Cleaning Basics

Keep this simple enough to repeat.

### Basic Steps

1. Wash your hands first.
2. Rinse produce well under clean running water.
3. For firmer produce, use a dedicated produce brush where useful.
4. For some fruits and vegetables, a simple baking soda soak can be one practical option before a final rinse.
5. Dry and store properly so food lasts longer.

### Important Principle

Do not let cleaning become so complicated that you avoid buying produce at all.
Better is better.
Consistent is better than elaborate and rare.

## Storage That Reduces Waste

A lot of healthy buying fails in the storage stage.

### Refrigerate Soon

- leafy greens
- berries
- herbs
- yogurt
- cut vegetables

### Often Fine On The Counter First

- bananas
- avocados until ripe
- tomatoes, depending on use and ripeness
- citrus for short periods

### Often Better In A Cool Dry Place

- onions
- garlic
- potatoes
- sweet potatoes

### Practical Tips

- wash only what you are likely to use soon if moisture will shorten life
- freeze extra fruit for blending
- freeze herbs, greens, or chopped vegetables before they turn
- keep visible foods visible so they are more likely to be used

## Blender Buying Guide

Do not buy a blender for status.
Buy for actual use.

### What Matters Most

- durability
- ease of cleaning
- jar size that suits your life
- blade and motor strong enough for what you plan to blend
- replacement part availability
- warranty that is actually worth something

### Budget Tiers

#### Basic

Best for:

- softer fruits
- lighter blends
- one-person use

Watch for:

- weak motors
- short lifespan
- poor lid fit

#### Mid-Range

Best for:

- regular daily use
- simple family use
- better long-term value

Often the best balance for many readers.

#### Stronger Tier

Best for:

- heavy use
- tougher ingredients
- larger household use

Useful, but not necessary for everyone.

### Used Or Refurbished

This can be a smart move when:

- the model is known to be durable
- parts are replaceable
- the price gap is meaningful

## Simple Preparation Habits

You do not need a perfect kitchen system.
You need a few habits that lower friction.

Helpful examples:

- fill water containers the night before
- wash and prep one or two foods after shopping
- freeze blend ingredients in small portions
- keep one fallback meal and one fallback blend in mind
- clean the blender quickly after use so it stays easy to reach for

## When To Shop

This varies by store, but a few practical principles help:

- shop after a simple plan is made
- avoid buying while rushed, hungry, or emotionally scattered
- use one weekly rhythm when possible
- look for markdown patterns in stores you use regularly
- frozen and shelf-stable items can reduce pressure between fresh-food trips

## Reset After A Hard Week

If you fall off, return to the basics.

Start with:

- water
- one simple meal
- one simple blend
- one cleaner shopping trip
- one early night

Do not restart with punishment.
Restart with structure.
